## 3. High-Priority Faculty Viva Questions & Authoritative Defenses

### Question 1: "Why not use a standard supervised deep learning model like a 3D U-Net or SwinUNETR?"
> **Authoritative Defense:**  
> Supervised networks require tens of thousands of paired clean/corrupted volumes for training. In specialized modalities—such as custom CACTI cameras, rare clinical PET/CT pathologies, or satellite hyperspectral sensors—ground-truth clean 3D volumes are physically unobtainable or prohibitively expensive. DeepTensor solves inverse problems **self-supervised on the single corrupted instance**, requiring zero training datasets. Furthermore, supervised models hallucinate features when exposed to out-of-distribution noise, whereas DeepTensor enforces exact measurement consistency with the forward physics operator $\mathcal{A}(X)$.

### Question 2: "Deep Image Prior is notorious for overfitting to noise if left running too long. How did you control this?"
> **Authoritative Defense:**  
> We implemented three strict safeguards:
> 1. *Low-Rank Tensor Bottleneck:* Unlike standard 2D DIP where the network reconstructs the full image directly, DeepTensor forces reconstruction through low-rank factor contraction ($U V$ or $u \otimes v \otimes w$). A rank of $R=20$ or $R=32$ acts as an insurmountable dimensional bottleneck against arbitrary high-frequency noise.
> 2. *Latent Perturbation Regularization:* At every optimization step, input latents are perturbed with Gaussian jitter ($z + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$), acting as stochastic noise smoothing on the parameter landscape.
> 3. *Exponential Moving Average (EMA):* We maintain an exponential running average of reconstructed tensors ($\alpha = 0.99$), dampening high-frequency epoch-to-epoch variance.

### Question 3: "Did you actually implement Tucker decomposition, or is it only discussed in your literature review?"
> **Authoritative Defense:**  
> We distinguish this with complete scientific transparency:
> - Classical truncated Tucker decomposition **is fully implemented** in `src/deeptensor/baselines/spectral.py` (lines 39–60) via TensorLy and executed as a numerical benchmark.
> - However, a **deep generative Tucker prior** (learning a dense neural 3D core tensor $\mathcal{G} \in \mathbb{R}^{R_1 \times R_2 \times R_3}$) was deliberately omitted from the deep prior pipeline. A dense core scales cubically $O(R^3)$. For our high-rank testbeds like Yale Face B ($R=2000$) and PET/CT ($R=1000$), a dense Tucker core would require 1 to 8 billion parameters, exceeding GPU memory capacity. CP decomposition was chosen because its diagonal core scales linearly ($O(R)$).

### Question 4: "Why did Baseline CNN outperform Transformer on ICVL and Video, but underperform on Yale Face B?"
> **Authoritative Defense:**  
> This highlights **Application-Dependent Architectural Performance** for untrained neural generative priors:
> - In ICVL, the 260 channels represent continuous electromagnetic wavelengths where physical absorption curves vary smoothly. In Video CS, adjacent frames obey temporal velocity continuity. 1D convolutions possess a localized receptive field and translation equivariance, enforcing local physical smoothness and filtering high-frequency noise.
> - In Yale Face B, the 160 dimensions correspond to discrete azimuthal and elevation lighting angles. Light changes create non-local, global photometric interactions (e.g., cast shadows and specular reflections across the face). Convolutions assume translational smoothness across lighting angles, whereas Pure Transformer's unconstrained pairwise attention can flexibly capture global photometric couplings. However, we treat these inductive bias distinctions as empirical hypotheses grounded in coordinate physics, rather than sweeping universal laws.

### Question 5: "Why did Pointwise Control outperform both CNN and Transformer on PET/CT (30.62 dB)?"
> **Authoritative Defense:**  
> In 3D tomographic CT/PET z-axis slices (56 slices), each slice represents a distinct anatomical cross-section (e.g., moving from lung tissue to diaphragm to liver). Enforcing convolutional translation invariance across slices physically blurs distinct organs together. Pure Transformer attention adds unnecessary pairwise cross-talk degrees of freedom. Pointwise Control provides non-linear channel capacity *strictly within individual slices without enforcing cross-slice spatial coupling*, allowing anatomical boundaries to decouple naturally.

### Question 6: "In your Adaptive Rank Selection, how do you ensure that the rank growth decision does not leak clean ground-truth information?"
> **Authoritative Defense:**  
> Clean ground-truth data ($X_{\text{clean}}$) is strictly sequestered and used solely for post-hoc PSNR/SSIM evaluation. In `run_figure9_adaptive.py` (lines 64–140), the rank-growth trigger monitors **strictly the self-supervised $\mathcal{L}_1$ measurement loss against the noisy observation $y$**. When the relative improvement in the EMA-smoothed self-supervised loss drops below $10^{-3}$ over a 200-epoch window, a plateau is declared and rank is expanded. No clean data ever enters the optimization loop.

### Question 7: "What are the practical clinical or real-time limitations of DeepTensor?"
> **Authoritative Defense:**  
> The primary limitation is **computational latency during reconstruction**. Because DeepTensor optimizes network parameters for each individual scan, reconstructing a single 3D volume requires 1200 to 5000 epochs (approximately 2 to 8 minutes on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU). Unlike feed-forward supervised models (which perform inference in milliseconds), DeepTensor is not currently real-time. In our future work roadmap, we address this by preconditioning latent inputs with fast linear approximations (such as Filtered Backprojection for CT or pseudo-inverse for CACTI), which our research demonstrates can reduce convergence time by $5\times$ to $10\times$.
