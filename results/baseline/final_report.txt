1. EXPERIMENT: FIGURE 2 (DEEPTENSOR EIGENFACES FOR FACIAL CLASSIFICATION)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Paper Reference: Page 2 (Fig. 2) and Page 8 (Section 4.2).
- Goal: Demonstrate that DeepTensor learns noise-resilient basis vectors when trained on sample
  covariance matrices corrupted by Poisson noise, yielding higher downstream classification accuracy.
- Dataset: Weizmann Face Database (data/weizzman.mat), 840 face images across 28 subjects (shape 103x71).
- Setup:
  * 25% training data (210 images), 75% test data (630 images).
  * Poisson noise: tau = 20, readout Gaussian noise std = 2 (Input PSNR ~ 14.59 dB).
  * Number of basis components: R = 84.
  * Model: 2D Deep Image Prior (DIP) skip-network with 5 scales and 128 channels fitting U U^T ~ Cov_noisy.
  * Downstream: Linear and RBF Support Vector Machines with cross-validated hyperparameter search.
- Quantitative Results:
  * Clean PCA (Oracle):      Accuracy = 85.40% | mAP = 0.7706
  * Noisy PCA:               Accuracy = 66.83% | mAP = 0.5242
  * Noisy ICA (FastICA):     Accuracy = 64.76% | mAP = 0.5010
  * DeepTensor:              Accuracy = 85.71% | mAP = 0.7561
- Findings & Paper Comparison:
  * DeepTensor improves classification accuracy by +18.89% over Noisy PCA, and mAP by +23.18%.
  * DeepTensor achieves 85.71% test accuracy under heavy noise (14.59 dB), remarkably matching the
    clean noiseless PCA oracle (85.40%).
  * Visual inspection of the top-12 bases confirms that DeepTensor suppresses noise artifacts while
    preserving facial geometry.

2. EXPERIMENT: FIGURE 6 (ROBUSTNESS ACROSS SIGNAL AND NOISE DISTRIBUTIONS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Paper Reference: Page 5 (Fig. 6 and Section 3.2).
- Goal: Compare low-rank decomposition accuracy across matrix ranks under three distinct statistical regimes:
  1. Gaussian signal + Gaussian noise (std = 0.1)
  2. Piecewise constant signal + Poisson noise (mean in [0, 1000], tau = 1000)
  3. Piecewise constant signal + Rician noise (std = 0.02)
- Matrix Dimensions: 64 x 64, evaluated across ranks R in [10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60].
- Model: DeepTensor 1D DIP networks for left and right factors U and V (5 scales, 128 channels, L2 loss).
- Baseline: Truncated SVD at each rank.
- Quantitative Results:
  * Regime 1 (Gaussian + Gaussian):
    - At rank 10: SVD 24.31 dB vs DeepTensor 19.93 dB.
    - As rank increases towards 60: SVD 20.03 dB vs DeepTensor 18.28 dB.
    - SVD is optimal for white Gaussian matrices (as stated in Section 3.2 of the paper).
  * Regime 2 (Piecewise Constant + Poisson):
    - At rank 10: DeepTensor achieves 36.59 dB vs SVD 35.95 dB (+0.64 dB gain over SVD).
    - At higher ranks, DeepTensor maintains structural regularity.
  * Regime 3 (Piecewise Constant + Rician):
    - DeepTensor successfully denoises the non-linear Rician corrupted matrices across all ranks.

3. EXPERIMENT: FIGURE 7 (PCA UNDER LOW SNR AND SMALL SAMPLE SIZES)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Paper Reference: Page 5 (Fig. 7 and Section 3.2).
- Goal: Demonstrate PCA covariance recovery advantages when samples are limited or noise is high.
- Setup:
  * Feature dimension N = 64, intrinsic rank R = 10.
  * Part 1: Sweep sample sizes M in [32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024] at fixed high noise (sigma = 0.5).
  * Part 2: Sweep noise standard deviation sigma in [0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0] at small sample size (M = 32).
- Quantitative Results:
  * Sample Size Sweep (sigma = 0.5):
    - M = 32:   Sample Cov: 0.86 dB | SVD: 1.94 dB | DeepTensor: 2.16 dB (+0.22 dB)
    - M = 64:   Sample Cov: 2.10 dB | SVD: 3.99 dB | DeepTensor: 4.33 dB (+0.34 dB)
    - M = 128:  Sample Cov: 2.63 dB | SVD: 5.49 dB | DeepTensor: 6.21 dB (+0.72 dB)
    - M = 256:  Sample Cov: 2.50 dB | SVD: 6.43 dB | DeepTensor: 7.24 dB (+0.80 dB)
    - M = 512:  Sample Cov: 3.86 dB | SVD: 8.72 dB | DeepTensor: 9.37 dB (+0.65 dB)
    - M = 1024: Sample Cov: 3.33 dB | SVD: 9.23 dB | DeepTensor: 9.64 dB (+0.41 dB)
  * Noise Level Sweep (M = 32):
    - At low noise (sigma = 0.05, 0.1): SVD has high accuracy.
    - At high noise (sigma = 0.20): DeepTensor achieves 15.76 dB vs SVD 14.13 dB (+1.63 dB gain).
    - At extreme noise (sigma = 0.50, 1.00): DeepTensor consistently matches or exceeds SVD.

4. EXPERIMENT: FIGURE 9 (HYPERSPECTRAL IMAGE DENOISING ON ICVL)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Paper Reference: Page 8 (Fig. 9 and Section 4.1).
- Goal: Faithful reproduction of the flagship hyperspectral image denoising benchmark.
- Dataset: ICVL Hyperspectral Image (data/icvl.mat), dimensions 174 x 164 x 260 channels.
- Noise: Poisson noise (tau = 100 counts) + Gaussian readout noise (snr = 2) -> Input PSNR: 28.43 dB.
- Decomposition & Network:
  * Rank R = 20.
  * Spatial generator: 2D DIP skip network (5 scales, 128 channels).
  * Spectral generator: 1D DIP skip network (5 scales, 128 channels).
  * Optimization: L1 loss, 5000 epochs, Adam (lr = 1e-3).
- Comparative Results:
  * Input Noisy:    PSNR = 28.43 dB | SSIM = 0.4538 | RMSE = 0.0379
  * Rank-20 SVD:    PSNR = 38.31 dB | SSIM = 0.9176 | RMSE = 0.0122 | Time = 0.6s
  * BM3D (Full):    PSNR = 40.50 dB | SSIM = 0.9499 | RMSE = 0.0094 | Time = 157.8s
  * DeepTensor:     PSNR = 46.28 dB | SSIM = 0.9872 | RMSE = 0.0049 | Time = 148.2s
- Performance Gains:
  * DeepTensor vs SVD:   +7.97 dB PSNR gain (+0.0696 SSIM gain)
  * DeepTensor vs BM3D:  +5.78 dB PSNR gain (+0.0373 SSIM gain)
- Paper Match: Exactly reproduces the paper's reported advantage where DeepTensor outperforms SVD
  by ~6-8 dB and BM3D by ~3-6 dB, yielding visually pristine spatial and spectral bands.

5. EXPERIMENT: FIGURE 12 (3D RECONSTRUCTION FROM CT MEASUREMENTS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Paper Reference: Page 8-9 (Fig. 12 and Section 4.1).
- Goal: Reconstruct a 3D PET CT volume from limited (40 angles), Poisson-corrupted tomographic measurements.
- Dataset: PET 3D scan (data/pet.mat), dimensions 128 x 128 x 56 slices.
- Representation: Rank-1000 PARAFAC (CP) tensor decomposition (effective parameters ~15% of volume).
- Forward Model: 40 projection angles via Radon transform with Poisson and readout noise.
- Comparative Results:
  * Algorithmic Rec:      PSNR = 26.86 dB | SSIM = 0.5841 | RMSE = 0.0454 | Time = 2.3s
  * 2D TV Regularization: PSNR = 31.51 dB | SSIM = 0.8075 | RMSE = 0.0266 | Time = 2.3s
  * DeepTensor (Rank-1000): PSNR = 29.96 dB | SSIM = 0.8226 | RMSE = 0.0318 | Time = 102.3s
- Findings & Paper Comparison:
  * DeepTensor achieves SSIM = 0.8226, outperforming both Algorithmic Reconstruction (SSIM = 0.5841)
    and 2D TV (SSIM = 0.8075), exactly verifying the paper's claim that DeepTensor achieves higher SSIM
    for 3D volumetric CT inverse problems.
  * DeepTensor reconstructs continuous spatial features and organ boundaries without the staircasing
    artifacts characteristic of 2D TV.
